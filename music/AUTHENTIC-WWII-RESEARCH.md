# Phase 23C.6 — Authentic WWII Music Research

**Goal:** Increase the radio's genuine 1939–1945 cultural authenticity with real period performances or recordings; no synthetic imitations.

**Current findings:** The Library of Congress Recording Registry identifies real 1943–1945 musical performances, including Stan Kenton's 1943 **Artistry in Rhythm**, Oran Page's 1944 **Uncle Sam Blues** (V-Disc), Johnny Mercer's 1944 **Ac-Cent-Tchu-Ate the Positive**, and Louis Jordan's 1945 **Caldonia**. These are excellent examples of the desired sound, **but are not presently eligible for website distribution** because a specific free license, public-domain exception, or rights-holder permission was not verified for the recording and work. Publication before 1950 is NOT sufficient. See https://www.loc.gov/collections/national-jukebox/about-this-collection/rights-and-access/ .

**Promising actual wartime government material:** LOC Citizen DJ's curated 1943/1944 government film recordings explicitly permit commercial reuse. Examples include *When Work Is Done* (1944), *Film Communique No. 4* (1943), and *The Memphis Belle* (1944). However, the source must be screened for **recognizable complete instrumental/song performances**, narration must not be mistaken for songs, and any independently protected incorporated composition must be ruled out. Do not indiscriminately add film audio to the radio. Source rights https://citizen-dj.labs.loc.gov/loc-national-screening-room/use/ .

**Wartime national archival sources:** NARA identifies real WWII radio broadcasts and performance recordings, but some items have asterisked copyright or donor restrictions: https://www.archives.gov/research/military/ww2/sound-recordings.html . The U.S. Air Force band performed 1942–45 and had wartime broadcasts and recordings, but no freely redistributable exact file has yet been independently established: https://www.music.af.mil/USAFBand/About-Us/History/A-Wartime-Birth-1941-1945/ .

## Release gate
1. Find an exact real historical audio file, not a synthetic rendition or generic archive listing.
2. Confirm it's a full musical performance (or intentionally scoped radio ID), not a 30-second excerpt, spoken report or underlying film dialogue.
3. Document both composition and recording/transfer reuse rights; use public domain or free commercial reuse only.
4. Record original performer, exact recording date, source file download URL, attribution and checksum.
5. If any required evidence is missing: **skip it** and research a different candidate without requiring the site owner to make individual licensing decisions.
6. Only verified eligible, quality-appropriate files may be committed to `audio/` and `audio-manifest.json`.

**Status:** Research completed; **zero 1940s-specific musical recordings deployed** by this phase. Existing 27 authentic pre-1923 recordings remain on air unmodified. Caution: the word "authentic" refers to original historical performance, not necessarily one recorded during WWII.
