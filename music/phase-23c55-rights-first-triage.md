# Phase 23C.55 — Rights-first source triage for underserved TOBS stations

**Status:** development research and verification. No new audio approved; no change to playable manifest or production. Sweden remains frozen.

## Confirmed constraints from primary sources

1. The Library of Congress Recorded Sound Research Center warns that historic sound recordings remain subject to US copyright protection and that archival possession does not itself confer rights to copy or redistribute. See https://guides.loc.gov/recorded-sound-research/using-the-library/sound-recordings
2. The LOC Traditional Music and Spoken Word Card Catalog covers 1897–1962, mostly 1933–1950, but explicitly says the digitized cards are **not** the audio recordings, and only a fraction of recordings can be heard online: https://www.loc.gov/collections/traditional-music-and-spoken-word/about-this-collection/
3. LOC Citizen DJ National Jukebox samples are expressly cleared for reuse, but the described recordings are **pre-1923**; these do not fulfill the original 1936–1945 performance goal: https://citizen-dj.labs.loc.gov/loc-jukebox-popular/use/
4. A Commons file named after a 1940 composition can actually be a **modern recording**: e.g. 'This Land is Your Land.ogg' is dated 2007 on its file page, so it must not be classified as a 1940 original: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:This_Land_is_Your_Land.ogg

## Actionable selection criteria

- Search archive items for Canada, Australia, Romania, Belgium and the Philippines first.
- For each candidate, record title, performer, **recording** date, exact playable file, original archive page, recording rights, and website streaming/reuse permission.
- If a source is merely a catalog card, film, excerpt, CD rip, modern cover, or undownloadable player, keep it in research; do not count it as approved.
- Distinguish a verified source URL from a track that has passed actual runtime playback tests.
- Preserve the 65 station mapping and freeze Sweden.
